# Four Pillars USA — Design Direction

## Reference specification

The provided Four Pillars USA / Daniel for America website is the ground-truth reference for this rebuild. Preserve its editorial campaign character: a dark, cinematic landscape hero; warm ivory policy sections; red-rose emphasis; small uppercase field-file labels; serif display typography; thin rules; restrained motion; and a public-record / policy-brief tone. Extend the framework with a new primary pillar titled **Equality** and make the uploaded report a clearly accessible downloadable resource.

## Selected approach: Archive at Dawn

### Design Movement
Contemporary editorialism shaped by Swiss information design, archival campaign ephemera, and quiet civic modernism.

### Core Principles
1. Treat policy as a public record: labels, chapter markers, dates, and metadata should feel precise and inspectable.
2. Pair a dark, atmospheric opening with warm paper-like reading surfaces so urgency gives way to thoughtfulness.
3. Use asymmetry and long horizontal rules to create a field-file rhythm rather than a generic centered landing page.
4. Keep interaction restrained and purposeful: movement should reveal structure, not decorate it.

### Color Philosophy
The palette uses midnight blue-black for seriousness and trust, warm parchment for civic clarity, and a deep rose signal for human stakes and action. Rose is reserved for emphasis, active states, and the Equality pillar so the new material feels ownable without breaking the existing campaign language.

### Layout Paradigm
A document-like vertical narrative with a pinned field-file rail, offset editorial columns, large chapter numerals, and wide policy cards. Major sections should alternate between full-bleed atmosphere and tactile paper surfaces; avoid a uniform centered grid.

### Signature Elements
1. Hairline rules with tiny uppercase metadata labels.
2. Oversized serif headlines with italicized, rose-highlighted words.
3. A persistent chapter navigator that treats pillars as an unfolding public record.

### Interaction Philosophy
Every interactive element should feel like opening a file, turning a page, or following a public record. Buttons use clear directional language. Hover states brighten rules, shift arrows, or lift a document edge; they do not rely on heavy glow or bouncing.

### Animation
Use short, ease-out transitions for button presses and navigation. Reveal chapter cards with a subtle upward fade and stagger only when entering the viewport. Let the hero background drift almost imperceptibly. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` by removing transforms and background drift.

### Typography System
Use **Cormorant Garamond** for display headlines and **DM Sans** for interface text, labels, body copy, and metadata. Headlines are large, high-contrast, and occasionally italicized. Metadata is uppercase with expanded tracking. Body copy remains compact and readable, with a generous line height.

### Brand Essence
A civic campaign for Americans who want practical systems, equal treatment, and a country confident enough to protect both security and conscience.

Personality: **measured, humane, exacting**.

### Brand Voice
Headlines should be declarative and memorable; CTAs should be direct and public-minded; microcopy should identify what is proposed, what is sourced, and what remains under review.

Example lines:

> Equal protection. Religious freedom. One American promise.

> Read the report behind the pillar.

Avoid generic filler such as “Welcome to our website” or “Get started today.”

### Wordmark & Logo
Use a compact four-quadrant seal built from four offset architectural bars, with one rose quadrant for Equality. The mark should appear beside the stacked wordmark “FOUR PILLARS USA / DANIEL FOR AMERICA” and remain legible as a favicon-sized symbol.

### Signature Brand Color
**Four Pillars Rose — #D56B78.** It signals the human dimension of public policy and distinguishes action without becoming partisan neon.

## Equality pillar content direction

The new Equality pillar will be titled **Equality** throughout navigation, chapter cards, section labels, and calls to action. Its core message is that equal treatment under law and religious freedom should be harmonized rather than ranked. The pillar will include a concise on-page overview, three principles—equal treatment, freedom of conscience, and responsible accommodation—and a download card for “Equality in America: Balancing Equal Treatment Under Law with Religious Freedom,” dated August 30, 2026.

## Style decisions

- Keep the site reference-matched rather than introducing a new visual language.
- Use rose emphasis for Equality but retain the existing dark navy / parchment / white structure.
- Keep the Equality report link visible both within the Equality pillar and within the Official Materials area.
- Label the report as a research resource and note that it focuses primarily on federal law and summarizes developments through August 2026.
